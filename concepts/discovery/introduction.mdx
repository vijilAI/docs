[Discover](/concepts/platform/discover) has four components. Every page in the Discover documentation is written in terms of them, so it is worth ten minutes to fix them in your head before you install anything.

**Target → Collector → Resource → Classification**

| Component | One line |
|-----------|----------|
| **[Target](/concepts/discovery/target)** | A place you want looked at |
| **[Collector](/concepts/discovery/collector)** | The software that looks |
| **[Resource](/concepts/discovery/resource)** | A single thing found, with its evidence |
| **[Classification](/concepts/discovery/classification)** | The verdict on whether that thing is AI |

## How They Relate

You define a Target for each place worth inventorying. You install one or more Collectors that report into it. Each Collector returns Resources. Each Resource carries a Classification and the evidence behind it.

The relationship that surprises people is the one between Targets and Collectors: it is one to many. A fleet of two hundred laptops is **one** Target with two hundred Collectors reporting into it, not two hundred Targets. Findings group under the Target, which is what makes the fleet readable as a fleet.

## Why This Split Matters

Separating the place from the software that looks at it is what lets coverage be honest. A Target with no Collector reports nothing, and Discover says so rather than showing you a clean result. A Target whose Collector lost a network route reports a blind spot rather than an empty finding list.

An inventory that cannot distinguish "nothing is there" from "nothing could be seen" is worse than no inventory, because it reads the same as good news.
